## Principle 1 — Coach the Journey

### Coach — 3 Key Points
- Think across seasons, not just about the current year.
- Skills compound: things that are hard at first eventually become normal.
- The goal is not just to build this year’s robot — it is to build next year’s team.

### Student Story Reminder
- Early years: SPIKE block coding was difficult; by the final FLL season the team was writing Python and preparing for FTC.

---

## Principle 2 — Play the Whole Game

### Coach — 3 Key Points
- Robot Game can easily consume the season because it is visible and exciting.
- FLL includes Robot Game, Robot Design, Innovation Project, and Core Values.
- Some of the most memorable learning experiences happen away from the competition table.

### Student Story Reminder
- SUBMERGED: Phil brought an underwater ROV to the dock; Jon dropped his keys 20 feet underwater; the team helped recover them while a crab tried to grab them.

---

## Principle 3 — Start With an Expert

### Coach — 3 Key Points
- Instead of asking kids to invent a problem, start by finding someone who works in the season’s topic area.
- Ask experts about real frustrations, inefficiencies, and recurring problems.
- A good expert visit can lead directly from observation to a meaningful problem.

### Student Story Reminder
- SUPERPOWERED: Benton Rural Electric Association gave the team a solar farm tour. Ron Mitchell explained that dusty panels had not been cleaned in about two years and the dust reduced energy production.

---

## Principle 4 — Diversify Your Experts

### Coach — 3 Key Points
- Don’t interview five people with essentially the same expertise.
- Different experts can contribute different pieces of a solution.
- Innovation often comes from combining ideas from unrelated fields.

### Student Story Reminder
- MASTERPIECE: trail master identified the need for community support; cosplay artist brought interactive electronics; another artist brought sound and light — combined into an interactive hiking-themed art display.

---

## Principle 5 — Go Physical

### Coach — 3 Key Points
- Physical prototypes help younger students think, test, and learn more concretely.
- Building something real exposes engineering problems that ideas on paper do not.
- Let students touch, change, break, rebuild, deploy, and test their solution.

### Student Story Reminder
- SUBMERGED: PVC test rig, 3D-printed sensors, UV lights, waterproofing, buoyancy, ocean deployment, and a disgusting layer of biofilm after three weeks.

---

## Principle 6 — Wow the Judges

### Coach — 3 Key Points
- Think “analog PowerPoint”: large visuals, cue cards, props, models, and prototypes.
- Give judges something they can see, touch, or manipulate.
- The goal is clarity and memorability, not gimmicks.

### Student Story Reminder
- SUBMERGED: judges physically bent the team’s flexible UV-light prototype around different 3D-printed sensor shapes.

---

## Principle 7 — Work Backwards

### Coach — 3 Key Points
- Start with the mission interaction, not with navigation or code.
- Push the chassis to the mission by hand and prove the mechanism first.
- Separating manipulation from navigation makes failures easier to diagnose and iteration much faster.

### Student Story Reminder
- SUBMERGED Coral Tree: first tried scissoring it up from the south, then rotating from the east; finding the exact pivot point turned one of the least reliable missions into a nearly 100% reliable one.

---

## Principle 8 — 2:30s

### Coach — 3 Key Points
- Practice the entire 2-minute-30-second Robot Game, not just individual missions.
- Track mission success and transition times so the team can see patterns instead of guessing.
- Match performance depends on the whole system: robot, code, mechanisms, technicians, setup, and recovery.

### Student Story Reminder
- Data showed late-match missions were weaker, especially “Send over the submersible.” Tracking transition times also revealed where technicians needed to coordinate faster.

---

## Principle 9 — Parent Showcase

### Coach — 3 Key Points
- Parents often cannot appreciate the details of Robot Game from the bleachers.
- Put families close to the field and let them watch a full 2:30 match.
- The showcase helps parents understand the engineering, programming, and teamwork behind what the students do.

### Student Story Reminder
- Mrs. Zink used to say Bianca was in a “LEGO club.” After seeing the robot up close at Parent Showcase, she started telling people Bianca was on a robotics team.

---

## Principle 10 — Mock Judging

### Coach — 3 Key Points
- Practice the full judging flow, including presentations and difficult Q&A.
- Parents should act as judges and push the team with the questions they least want to be asked.
- Don’t coach during the session; give feedback after the full mock judging experience.

### Student Story Reminder
- SUBMERGED experiment leaked and the UV light failed. A parent asked the hardest possible question about the failure, helping the team practice explaining what failed, what they learned, and how experts helped them understand waterproofing.

- A team from Spain loaned them a motor; the Mavericks repaired the robot and recovered.

## Principles to Take Home

### Coach — 3 Key Points
- Review all 10 principles quickly.
- Coaches do not need to use every idea — even two or three useful takeaways are enough.
- Improvement compounds over time for both students and coaches.
